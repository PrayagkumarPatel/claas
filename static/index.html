Frequently asked questions

Is real hardware connected to this system?

No. This MVP is a high-fidelity simulation of the marketplace coordination layer. The Central Limit Order Book, matching engine, and price discovery mechanisms are fully functional production code, while liquidity is driven by synthetic bots. A production deployment incorporates cryptographic workload verification, container orchestration, and escrow payment settlement.

How is the live spot price calculated?

Spot price represents the clearing price of the most recently executed trade on the order book. When bids and asks cross, trades settle at the maker's limit price, establishing transparent market-clearing levels governed entirely by supply and demand.

What is a GPU-hour?

One GPU-hour equals one GPU allocated for sixty minutes. For instance, an 8-GPU node running a 30-minute test run consumes 4 GPU-hours. It serves as the standardized commodity unit that enables heterogeneous hardware architectures to trade on a unified order book.

Why does the spot price fluctuate in cycles?

The simulation compresses a 24-hour enterprise training cycle into 3 real-world minutes. You can observe demand rise during peak hours as startup bots submit urgent batch training bids, and soften during off-peak periods.

Are transactions and order state stored in a database?

The matching engine maintains an in-memory limit order book to maximize throughput and minimize latency. Your client-side orders are tracked within your browser session.
